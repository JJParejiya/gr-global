Join us

Build a career in international trade

Freight forwarding is a trade you learn by doing it. If you are careful with detail and steady under a deadline, we will teach you the rest.

Why here

A small team, real responsibility

There are eight of us handling documentation, procurement, accounts and the warehouse floor. That size is deliberate. It means nobody spends a year shadowing someone else before they touch a live file.

New joiners sit with the consignments from the first week. You will learn classification because you will be doing it, and you will learn how a customs desk actually works because you will be talking to one.

We hire for care rather than for a CV. A bill of lading with a wrong number costs a client days at a port, so attention to detail matters here more than a qualification does.

If that sounds like the way you work, there is a place for you on the team, whether or not one of the roles below has your exact job title on it.

What you get

More than a desk in Bhuj

Nothing here is a perk in the table-tennis sense. These are the things people who work in freight actually care about.

  • Learn the whole trade

    Classification, documentation, customs procedure and carrier negotiation. Not one narrow slice of it, repeated for years.

  • A path to responsibility

    People here move from assisting to owning a client file. How fast depends on you, not on a promotion cycle.

  • Direct access

    No layers between you and the decision. If something needs changing, you raise it with the person who can change it.

  • International exposure

    Daily contact with carriers, brokers and buyers across fifteen countries, from a desk on the Bhuj-Mirjapar Highway.

  • Sane hours

    Vessels do not keep office hours, but that is what a rota is for. Late nights are the exception, not the culture.

  • Recognition that is specific

    Good work gets named, not averaged into a team score at the end of a quarter.

How we hire

Four steps, no games

You will know where you stand at every stage, and you will hear back either way.

  1. Send your details

    Use the form below or email us directly. Tell us which role interests you and what you have done that is relevant.

  2. A first conversation

    A call or a visit to the office. Mostly about how you work and what you want to learn, rather than a quiz on incoterms.

  3. A practical task

    Something close to the real job. A set of documents to check, or a consignment to plan. Paid if it takes more than an hour.

  4. Offer and start

    A written offer with the terms spelled out, and a first week sitting with live files rather than a manual.
