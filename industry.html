Delivering beyond borders

From basmati rice to bulldozers

Twelve export categories, each with its own packing rules, its own classification and its own clearance requirements. We hold that knowledge so you do not have to.

Sectors we move

A container is never just a container

Graded spices need ventilation and a phytosanitary certificate. Dyed yarn needs moisture control. A wheeled loader needs lashing calculations and an out-of-gauge booking. Electronics need a classification that survives inspection at the far end.

Every sector below carries a different set of rules, and getting them wrong costs time at the port rather than money on the invoice. These are the twelve we handle regularly, and the commodities inside each one that we see most often.

What does it take to get the agricultural industry going?

Grain, pulses, spices and oilseeds out of Gujarat and Rajasthan. These consignments live or die on paperwork: phytosanitary certificates, fumigation records and origin documents have to be right before the box is sealed, not after.

Yarn and fabric that arrives in the condition it left in

Cotton yarn, blended yarn and fabric rolls moving to converters across Asia and North Africa. Moisture is the enemy on a long sea leg, so packing specification and container selection matter as much as the sailing schedule.

Cars, trucks and two-wheelers, shipped and documented

Passenger cars, commercial vehicles and two-wheelers on roll-on roll-off or in containers. Battery disconnection, fuel levels and chassis documentation all have to match what the destination authority expects to see.

Every auto part component matters

High-value, high-volume and usually urgent. Aftermarket and OEM components move air when a production line is waiting and sea when it is not, and we consolidate smaller orders so the unit cost stays sensible.

Reliable and on-time construction machinery solutions

Excavators, concrete equipment, scaffolding and structural steel. Most of it is out of gauge, which means flat racks, lashing plans and a route survey before anything is loaded onto a trailer.

Heavy plant delivered to sites well inland from the port

Loaders, crushers, conveyor sections and the spares that keep them running. Mine sites are rarely near a port, so the road leg at destination is planned at the same time as the ocean leg.

Classification accuracy that keeps electronics moving

Consumer and industrial electronics, where classification accuracy decides the duty and a wrong code means an inspection. Lithium cells add their own dangerous goods declarations on the air leg.

Tiles, stone and fittings that survive the journey

Tiles, sanitaryware, natural stone and marble slabs. Heavy, fragile and unforgiving of poor crating, which is why the packing specification is agreed before the first slab is lifted.

Seasonal cargo that lands inside the window

Handicrafts, decorative items and corporate gifting, almost always tied to a season. Miss the window and the cargo is worth a fraction of its invoice, so transit time is the first thing we plan around.

The same booking, the same documents, every month

Packaged goods moving in volume on a repeating schedule. The work here is consistency: the same booking, the same documents and the same transit time every month, so the buyer can plan their shelves.

Health certificates, shelf life and reefer settings, confirmed

Processed foods, fresh produce and temperature-controlled consignments. Health certificates, shelf-life windows and reefer settings are checked and confirmed in writing before the container leaves.

Rigs, pumps and pipe for water and irrigation projects

Drilling rigs, submersible pumps, casing pipe and compressors going to water and irrigation projects. Long lengths and awkward weights, usually destined for sites well inland from the discharge port.
